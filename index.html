Core Philosophy

Product Judgment
over Faster Nonsense

A product architect understands what should be built, why it matters, how it gets built, and what happens if it fails. As AI collapses the distance between product, design, and engineering, you need fewer translators in the middle — and more people who can hold the whole system.

Software is getting cheaper to produce. That abundance is a trap if teams confuse speed with leverage. Fast nonsense is still nonsense. The scarce resource is judgment: knowing which problems are worth solving, especially where software decisions carry real-world consequences — energy, healthcare, transport, manufacturing, infrastructure.

- Daniel Arevalo, July 2026

How I Work

Product architecture for industrial AI

I help teams decide what is worth building, shape how it gets built, and own what happens if it fails — so speed compounds the right bets.

01

Decide

What is worth building

Engineering is no longer the bottleneck. I turn research, stakeholder tension, and domain constraint into a clear call on which problems deserve to exist as product.

02

Shape

How it gets built

I prototype in code, sit across design and engineering, and collapse the distance between decision and execution. Judgment without the ability to build is only half the job.

03

Own

What happens if it fails

Someone still has to care about the outcome. I stay with the system through launch and use — validating whether it works, and knowing when it is wrong.
